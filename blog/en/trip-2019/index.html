Munich by rail, 2019

The first of these trips, and the only one without a car: nine nights in a single Munich hotel in December 2019, with day returns to a monastery, a village brewery and Bamberg. The whole route fits in 730 kilometres of S-Bahn and ICE.

One hotel, four directions

The plan was to prove that beer travel does not need a car. Munich's S-Bahn network runs to the airport, to the lakes and, with a change, to the monastery at Andechs; the ICE reaches Bamberg in three hours twenty. Everything else was walking.

The hotel sat on Schillerstraße, two streets from the Hauptbahnhof, which turned out to matter more than any brewery on the list. Every day started and ended at the same door, and the suitcase never moved.

Airbräu at the airport was the first and last stop, which is a better idea than it sounds: a copper brewhouse inside the terminal, a beer garden under the roof, and a beer before the flight home that tastes like the trip is still going.

What the rail version costs you

Two of the three day trips are short and easy: Andechs is S-Bahn to Herrsching plus a bus or a forty-minute walk uphill; Aying is S-Bahn to Aying with the brewery a few hundred metres from the platform. Bamberg is the long one, and it is worth leaving at seven in the morning.

The trade is time. A car would have added Aying and Bamberg on the same day and cut the walking; without one, the day has a fixed shape and the beer is drunk at the pace the timetable allows. In December, with dark at half four, that turned out to be the right constraint.

Munich in Advent is a city of markets, and the beer gardens shut. The ones that stay open — Augustiner-Keller, Hofbräuhaus, Schneider — are full of people who are not tourists, which is the point of going in December at all.

What to order

  • Andechs Doppelbock Dunkel, at the monastery, after the walk up.
  • Ayinger Celebrator, in Aying, where it is a village beer rather than an import.
  • Schlenkerla Rauchbier in Bamberg, which is the reason the ICE day exists.
  • Augustiner Edelstoff at Marienplatz, first night, before anything else opens.

Where to eat

  • The roast pork and dumplings at Augustiner Bräustuben, next to the brewery on Landsberger Straße — the cheapest good meal of the trip.
  • Bread, butter and radishes at Andechs, with the beer, on the terrace if it is not raining.
  • Bamberg's smoked ham with horseradish, before the train back.

Notes for travellers

  • Buy the Bayern-Ticket for the Bamberg day if you are two or more; it covers the ICE only on regional trains, so check which service you are booking before you commit.
  • The S8 to the airport takes about forty minutes; the S1 is slower and less frequent. Leave more time than you think for the Airbräu stop.
  • December closes the beer gardens but not the cellars. Augustiner-Keller keeps its hall open, and it is warmer and quieter than the Hofbräuhaus.
  • Andechs is uphill from Herrsching; the bus runs but the path through the woods is the better half of the day.
