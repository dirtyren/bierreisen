The 2027 plan · Würzburg route

The trip this archive has been building towards: fifteen days from Frankfurt, two nights in Bruges and one in Ghent, the long day south split at Würzburg, two nights in Bamberg, then the Czech brewing towns and a Bavarian castle brewery before Munich. Thirty-one hundred kilometres of driving, all measured.

What changed from v1 and v2

This is the third version of the same two weeks. v1 ran the Belgian leg into the Czech one in a single 800-kilometre day; v2 added a night in Cologne, which split the drive but put a city between two better ones. v3 keeps the Belgian nights and splits the long day at Würzburg instead.

Würzburg is the right split because it is on the way and because the town is worth an evening: the Residenz, the old bridge with its wine drinkers, and Bürgerspital and Juliusspital making wine in a city that also brews beer.

The Franconian block is unchanged and deliberately so: two nights in Bamberg, the Schlenkerla–Spezial–Mahr's–Fässla walk, the drive out to Wagner in Merkendorf and Drei Kronen in Memmelsdorf, and one night at Maxlrain's castle brewery on the way south.

The numbers behind it

Every leg is routed rather than estimated: 2,305.6 km of driving over eleven legs, 26 h 53 behind the wheel, plus four brewery-crawl days that use no car at all — three of them in cities where the beer is within walking distance.

The single longest day is Bruges to Würzburg at just over five hours; the shortest is Bamberg to Sokolov. Nothing on the plan is a driving day disguised as a brewery day.

Fourteen nights, ten beds: that is more moving than 2024 and less than 2020, and the bets are that Bruges and Bamberg carry the two-night stays while the one-night stops are placed where the driving needs them.

What to order

  • De Halve Maan's Brugse Zot in Bruges, on the roof terrace if the weather allows.
  • Schlenkerla Fastenbier if the dates land in Lent — it is only on between Ash Wednesday and Easter.
  • Pilsner Urquell from the wood in Plzeň, and Budvar's tank beer the next day for the comparison.
  • Maxlrain's Schlossbier at the castle brewery, which is the quietest night of the trip.

Where to eat

  • Moules in Bruges or Ghent, in a room that has been serving them for fifty years.
  • Franconian Schäuferla in Bamberg, and the smoked ham in between breweries.
  • Czech roast pork with bread dumplings in Plzeň, at a pub with no English menu.

Notes for travellers

  • Bruges and Bamberg both reward the second night; if you have to cut one, cut a driving stop, not these.
  • Book Westvleteren and any Trappist visit weeks ahead; the reservation windows are narrow and not negotiable.
  • The German low-emission zones apply in Würzburg and Munich; make sure the rental has the green sticker.
  • Kout na Šumavě is appointment-only for groups and keeps odd hours: confirm the day before, not the week before.
