The first European loop, 2020

Seventeen days in February 2020, ten beds, and a loop that started as a list of breweries and ended as a way of travelling: Munich, Bamberg, Düsseldorf, the Trappists of Belgium, and a Swabian village brewery on the Danube on the way home. Two weeks later the borders closed.

The shape of it

The loop went north first: Bamberg for a night, then Düsseldorf for two, then across the Dutch border to La Trappe, down to Antwerp, and a week in Belgium with Brussels as the pivot. Bruges got a night, Westvleteren a detour, Chimay a night in the woods at Poteaupré.

The return was the part nobody plans well: Chimay to Zwiefaltendorf is five and a half hours, and the reward is a village brewery with rooms above the brewhouse and a dinner of Swabian things that have no equivalent anywhere else on the route.

It was February, so the beer gardens were shut and the towns were empty. Bruges in the rain at eight in the morning, with nobody on the Markt, is a different city from the one in the photographs, and better.

What it taught about driving

Ten beds in seventeen days is too many. The Belgian week worked because Brussels held for two nights; the German legs did not, and Bamberg in particular deserved the second night it did not get.

The car made the Trappist day possible — La Trappe, Westvleteren and Chimay are not connected by any sensible train — but it also made the trip a sequence of car parks. The next version cut the driving and added rail where the beer was in a city.

Germany and Belgium both enforce their low-emission zones; a rental without the right sticker is a fine waiting to happen. Check the Umweltzone before booking the car, not after.

What to order

  • Westvleteren XII, if you got through the reservation system — and the blonde and the 8 if you did not.
  • La Trappe Quadrupel at the abbey, with the cheese made by the monks.
  • Schlenkerla Rauchbier Märzen in Bamberg, the first night of the trip.
  • Altbier at Uerige, standing up, in the Altstadt.

Where to eat

  • Mussels in Brussels, in any brasserie that has been there longer than the menu trends.
  • Carbonnade flamande in Bruges, with the beer it was cooked in.
  • The abbey cheese board at La Trappe, which is the meal the Quadrupel was built for.

Notes for travellers

  • Westvleteren sells only by reservation, by phone or through the abbey's own system, with a per-person limit; it is not a brewery you turn up at.
  • The Belgian Trappists are spread across three provinces; plan them as a driving day or not at all.
  • Poteaupré, where you sleep at Chimay, is in the forest with nothing else around: eat at the auberge rather than driving out again.
  • February is cheap, quiet and cold. It is also when the smaller breweries take their holidays, so check opening days twice.
