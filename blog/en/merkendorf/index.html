Merkendorf

Merkendorf is a village of 924 people with two breweries, which is not remarkable in Upper Franconia and is the only reason to come. It joined Memmelsdorf in 1972 and sits east of it, with a church in the middle and fields on every side. Neither brewery is a museum: both pour in their own house and both cook.

Two breweries, nine hundred people

The village is a parish in its own right, with the church of Kreuzerhöhung standing in the middle of it, and a few streets of farmhouses around. There is a bus stop, a hall, and no shop worth the name. In a region where almost every village once brewed, Merkendorf kept two, and both still make beer in the building where they sell it.

Wagner, at Pointstraße 1, has brewed since 1797 and has belonged to the same family since 1919. The output is around 17,000 hectolitres a year, which makes it a substantial village brewery rather than a hobby: lorries leave for the region most mornings. The taproom is a few steps from the kettle, and the beer garden behind the house sits under old trees, with a playground beside it.

Hummel, at Lindenstraße 9, is the older of the two and the smaller. Beer has been brewed on the estate since 1556; the family bought it in 1846 and is now in the seventh generation. There is a Gastwirtschaft, a Bräustübla for about eighteen people, and a beer garden for the summer. It closes on Tuesdays, which is worth knowing before you get on a bus.

The village year

Two dates organise the year. Wagner's Kirchweih falls on the second Sunday after Assumption, in late August, and the Bockbieranstich on the Friday before Buß- und Bettag in November, when the dark Bock goes on tap and the taproom fills with the village. Both are ordinary Franconian customs, and both are a better reason to come than any weekend in between.

The rest of the year the village is quiet. You can walk the length of it in ten minutes, past the church and the last houses, out to where the fields begin and the orchards stand in rows. There is nothing to see, which is the point: the taproom is the sight, and the beer is brewed on the other side of the wall.

Getting there and eating

Bus 917 stops at Merkendorf (Memmelsd.) Kirche, which is as central as the village gets. There is no station, and the service is thin at weekends, so check the return times before the first beer rather than after. A car makes both breweries in one afternoon easy, and the drive from Bamberg takes about fifteen minutes. From Memmelsdorf the walk over the fields takes about forty.

Food is the reason to stay past one beer. Wagner cooks Franconian roasts and Klöße from a menu that changes daily, with Leberkäse on Wednesday and fish on Friday, and serves Brotzeiten all day. Hummel does roasts, a Fleischspieß the house is known for, and its own Kellerbier alongside. Neither is a destination restaurant; both are village kitchens that happen to brew.

What to order

  • Wagner Lagerbier Ungespunden, unfiltered and poured in the taproom a few steps from the brewhouse.
  • Wagner Kupferstich Rotbier, a red lager and the rarer style on this stretch of the road.
  • Hummel Kellerbier, low in carbonation and served young, which is the other village's answer.
  • Wagner Dunkler Bock from the November tapping, if your dates happen to line up with it.

Where to eat

  • Braten mit Klößen at Wagner, from a menu that changes daily: Leberkäse on Wednesday, fish on Friday.
  • The Fleischspieß at Hummel, the dish the house is known for, with the Kellerbier beside it.
  • A Brotzeit in either taproom: bread, butter, cheese, sausage, and no reason to hurry.

Notes for travellers

  • Bus 917 stops at Merkendorf (Memmelsd.) Kirche. Services are thin at weekends, so check the last return before you start.
  • Both breweries close at the start of the week: Wagner on Monday and Tuesday, Hummel on Tuesday.
  • Wagner has no rooms but will find you one in the village. The nearest hotel is the Drei Kronen in Memmelsdorf.
  • Both sell bottles at the house, which is how most of the village drinks their beer during the week.
  • Cash is the rule. Neither taproom is set up for a card in the way a city restaurant is.
