What to drink where

Nine beer addresses in Munich plus the day trips — the airport brewhouse, the monastery, the village taproom and the Rauchbier town, ranked by how far they are worth the train ride.

Munich

01

Airbräu

The brewery inside Munich Airport — a copper brewhouse in the terminal, with a beer garden under the roof. The first and last stop of the trip.

02

Augustiner

Augustiner beer at Marienplatz, in the middle of the historic centre.

03

Schneider Bräuhaus

Wheat-beer house on Tal: Aventinus and the original Tap 7.

04

Kloster Andechs

Benedictine monastery above the Ammersee — the Bräustüberl pours Doppelbock and a winter beer through the cold months.

05

Ayinger Bräustüberl

Aying, 32 km south-east by S-Bahn: the brewery's own taproom, Jahrhundertbier and the whole Ayinger range.

06

Hofbräuhaus

The room everyone has heard of — go early, stay for the brass band.

07

Schiller Bräu

Brewpub near the station: house beers brewed on site, a modern room and a young crowd.

08

Tap House Munich

Craft beer bar with a deep tap list — the antidote to a week of Helles.

09

Pfälzer Weinstube

Wine tavern at Odeonsplatz serving Palatinate wines — the one evening without beer.

10

Giesinger Bräustüberl

The Giesing brewery's taproom: unfiltered Helles and a neighbourhood crowd.

Bamberg

01

Schlenkerla

Rauchbier since 1405 — beechwood smoke, oak barrels, a vaulted cellar.

02

Spezial

The Bambergers' rauchbier, softer smoke than Schlenkerla.

03

Fässla

Zwergla dark lager, brewery tap opposite Spezial.

04

Mahr's Bräu

The 'aU' Ungespundetes — the reference Franconian lager.

05

Klosterbräu

Bamberg's oldest brewery; the Schwärzla is the one to order.

Andechs

01

Klosterbräustüberl Andechs

The monastery's own restaurant and beer hall — Doppelbock Dunkel, and a winter beer on tap from November.

Aying

01

Ayinger Bräustüberl

The brewery tap in the village: Jahrhundertbier, Altbairisch Dunkel, and the Ayinger range at source.

What this trip taught us

  • Late February is the quiet season: the brewery taps are open, the tourists are not, and the Alpine roads are empty.
  • Merkendorf is the deep end of Franconia: Wagner since 1797, a butcher who brews, and Bamberg eleven kilometres away.
  • Bad Reichenhall is the right base for three days of Alpine beer — Berchtesgaden is 18 km and Salzburg 23 km, so the car stays parked.
  • Two nights at Budvar beats one: the second morning is for the tour you could not get into the first day.
  • The 404 km Munich → Frankfurt leg on departure day is the longest of the trip; leave after breakfast and it is an easy afternoon.
