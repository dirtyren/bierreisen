📋 Complete Places Guide — Ranked & Curated

🏛️ Rome — The Eternal City

Days 1-3 & 13-14 · 5 total days · Arrive & depart here

⭐ Classic Historic Sites

1Colosseum + Roman Forum + Palatine Hill
The iconic symbol of Imperial Rome (70-80 AD). Combined ticket covers all three. Book the "Underground & Arena Floor" upgrade for restricted areas. Nothing captures the scale of Roman civilization like walking through these together.
Must-SeeBook Ahead
2The Pantheon
Best-preserved ancient Roman building (126 AD). The unreinforced concrete dome remains the world's largest after 2000 years. Stand under the oculus when it rains. Free entry (reservation required since 2023).
FreeEngineering Marvel
3Vatican Museums & Sistine Chapel
One of the world's greatest art collections. Michelangelo's ceiling, Raphael Rooms, Gallery of Maps. Book first-entry tickets (7:30 AM) or Friday night openings.
Book AheadHalf Day
4St. Peter's Basilica & Dome Climb
The largest church in the world. Michelangelo's Pietà, Bernini's baldachin. The dome climb offers Rome's best view. Free entry to basilica; dome ~10 EUR.
Free EntryViews
5Galleria Borghese
Bernini's Apollo & Daphne, Rape of Proserpina. Caravaggio, Titian, Raphael. Limited to 360 visitors per 2-hour slot. The most intimate world-class museum experience. MUST book weeks ahead.
Book Weeks AheadMasterpieces
6Castel Sant'Angelo
Hadrian's Mausoleum (139 AD) turned papal fortress turned museum. Connected to Vatican by secret passetto passage. 2000 years of layered history in one building.
Multi-era
7Piazza Navona
Built on Domitian's stadium (1st century AD). Bernini's Fountain of the Four Rivers. Rome's finest outdoor baroque ensemble. Best at dusk.
FreeEvening
8Trevi Fountain
Masterpiece of Roman baroque (1762). Visit at 6-7 AM for photos without crowds. Toss a coin to ensure your return to Rome.
Early Morning
9Via Appia Antica (Appian Way)
The "Queen of Roads" (312 BC). Walk or bike among ancient tombs, catacombs, and original paving stones. Rent bikes at the visitor center.
Half DayBike
10Trastevere
Medieval streets, Santa Maria in Trastevere (4th century). The most authentic "old Rome" atmosphere. Best explored on foot without a plan.
NeighborhoodEvening

💎 Hidden Gems

1Basilica di San Clemente
Three layers stacked: 12th-century basilica on a 4th-century church, on a 1st-century Mithraic temple. You literally descend through 2000 years. An underground river still flows at the lowest level.
Hidden GemMind-Blowing
2Quartiere Coppedè
A fairy-tale Art Nouveau neighborhood (1913-1926). Bizarre architecture mixing Gothic, Baroque, Greek, and medieval. Feels like a fantasy film set. Completely free, almost no tourists.
Hidden GemFree
3Aventine Keyhole + Orange Garden
Look through the Knights of Malta keyhole for a perfectly framed St. Peter's dome view. Nearby Orange Garden (Giardino degli Aranci) has the best free sunset viewpoint.
Hidden GemSunset
4Centrale Montemartini
Ancient Roman sculptures inside a decommissioned 1912 power plant. White marble against industrial machinery. Almost empty compared to Vatican Museums.
Hidden GemUnique
5Domus Romane di Palazzo Valentini
Underground excavations of Imperial-era Roman houses with multimedia projections. Walk on glass floors above original mosaics. Reservation required, ~12 EUR.
Hidden GemImmersive
6Protestant Cemetery
Final resting place of Keats, Shelley, Gramsci. One of Rome's most peaceful spots. Ancient Pyramid of Cestius (12 BC) rises beside it. Donation-based entry.
Hidden GemPeaceful
7Santo Stefano Rotondo
Rare circular church (5th century) with the most disturbing frescoes in Rome: 34 graphic martyrdom scenes. Hauntingly beautiful architecture. Almost no visitors.
Hidden GemUnusual
8Chiesa di Sant'Ignazio (Ceiling Illusion)
Andrea Pozzo's 1685 ceiling creates a perfect trompe-l'oeil dome — entirely painted flat. Stand on the marble disk in the nave. Free, almost never crowded.
Hidden GemFree

🍝 Food & Restaurants

1Roscioli (Salumeria con Cucina)
Via dei Giubbonari 21. Deli/restaurant hybrid. Famous for cacio e pepe, carbonara, incredible wine cellar. A food-lover's paradise. €€€ (40-70 EUR pp). Book ahead.
Fine Dining
2Da Enzo al 29
Trastevere. The definitive Roman classics — cacio e pepe, amatriciana, carciofi alla giudia. No reservations; arrive 30 min before opening. €€ (25-40 EUR pp).
Traditional
3Trapizzino (Testaccio)
Triangular pizza pocket stuffed with Roman fillings (chicken cacciatore, tripe, tongue). Invented here by Stefano Callegari. € (3-5 EUR each).
Street Food
4Pizzarium (Gabriele Bonci)
Near Vatican. Rome's best pizza al taglio. Legendary dough fermentation and creative toppings. Point at what looks good. € (5-12 EUR).
Pizza
5Armando al Pantheon
Family trattoria since 1961. Roman-Jewish cuisine: pasta e ceci, abbacchio, seasonal vegetables. Three generations. Book ahead. €€ (30-50 EUR pp).
Traditional
6Testaccio Market
The locals' market. Multiple stalls: Morini (stall 15) for supplì, Sergio (Box 16) for pasta. Go for lunch. Near Monte Testaccio (ancient pottery hill). € (8-15 EUR).
Market

🎨 Florence — Renaissance Capital

Days 4-6 · 3 days · The cradle of the Renaissance

⭐ Classic Historic Sites

1Galleria degli Uffizi
World's greatest Renaissance collection. Botticelli's "Birth of Venus" and "Primavera," Leonardo, Caravaggio, Raphael, Michelangelo. Allow 3-4 hours. Book months ahead.
Must-SeeBook Ahead
2Duomo + Brunelleschi's Dome Climb
Largest masonry dome ever built (1436), engineered without scaffolding. 463 steps for panoramic views. Interior frescoes by Vasari. Requires timed reservation.
Must-See463 Steps
3Galleria dell'Accademia (Michelangelo's David)
Home to the David (1501-1504). Also houses unfinished "Prisoners/Slaves" — arguably more moving, showing figures being freed from stone.
Must-SeeBook Ahead
4Palazzo Vecchio
Town hall since 1299. Vasari's massive frescoes in Salone dei Cinquecento. Secret passages tour reveals hidden Medici routes. Where Savonarola was held.
Secret Tour
5Basilica di Santa Croce
Final resting place of Michelangelo, Galileo, Machiavelli, Rossini. Giotto frescoes. The "Pantheon of Italian Glories."
Iconic
6Ponte Vecchio
Medieval bridge (1345) lined with goldsmiths. Vasari Corridor above. Only Florence bridge spared by Nazis in WWII. Best at golden hour.
FreeSunset
7Palazzo Pitti + Boboli Gardens
Medici residence. Palatine Gallery (Raphael, Titian, Rubens). Boboli is Florence's best escape from crowds — a Renaissance sculpture garden.
Half Day
8San Lorenzo + Medici Chapels
Medici family church by Brunelleschi. New Sacristy has Michelangelo's Dawn, Dusk, Night, Day sculptures. Profoundly moving.
Michelangelo

💎 Hidden Gems

1Museo di San Marco (Fra Angelico)
Former convent where Fra Angelico painted intimate frescoes in each monk's cell. The Annunciation at the top of the stairs is breathtaking. Rarely crowded. Savonarola's cell here too.
Hidden GemSpiritual
2Brancacci Chapel
Masaccio's frescoes (1425-1427) revolutionized Western painting. Michelangelo studied here. Limited to 30 visitors at a time. Reservations required.
Hidden GemRevolutionary Art
3Museo dell'Opera del Duomo
Ghiberti's original "Gates of Paradise," Donatello's Mary Magdalene, Michelangelo's Bandini Pietà. Dramatically undercrowded compared to Uffizi.
Hidden GemUncrowded
4Officina Santa Maria Novella
World's oldest pharmacy (1612, founded 1221 by Dominican friars). Still sells perfumes and liqueurs from original recipes. Museum-quality frescoed interior.
Hidden GemUnique
5Oltrarno Artisan Workshops
Traditional bookbinders, goldsmiths, leather workers, furniture restorers across the Arno. Via Maggio and Borgo San Frediano. Visit weekday mornings.
Hidden GemLiving Craft
6San Miniato al Monte
Stunning 1018 Romanesque church above Piazzale Michelangelo. Geometric marble facade. Gregorian chant at vespers (5:30 PM). Better views than the piazzale.
Hidden GemSunset

🍝 Food & Restaurants

1Trattoria Mario
Legendary since 1953 near San Lorenzo. Communal tables, cash only. Bistecca alla fiorentina, ribollita, peposo. Arrive 11:45 AM. Closes when food runs out.
Institution
2All'Antico Vinaio
Via dei Neri 74. Most famous schiacciata sandwiches in Florence. Porchetta, truffle cream, artichoke. 5-6 EUR for massive sandwiches. Line moves fast.
Street Food
3Trattoria Sostanza ("Il Troia")
Since 1869. Famous butter-poached chicken breast and artichoke omelet. Minimal decor, maximum flavor. Reservations essential. Cash only.
Historic
4Mercato Centrale (Ground Floor)
Skip the touristy upstairs. Ground floor = real market. Fresh produce, lampredotto carts. Da Nerbone for boiled beef sandwiches since 1872.
Market
5Lampredotto Cart (Street Food)
THE true Florentine street food — tripe sandwich from mobile carts. L'Antico Trippaio (Piazza dei Cimatori) or Il Trippaio di San Frediano. ~4 EUR.
Street FoodAuthentic

🏗️ Pisa — Beyond the Tower

Day 6 (afternoon) · Day trip from Florence · 1 hour by train

⭐ Historic Sites

1Camposanto Monumentale
13th-century cloister cemetery with soil from Golgotha. Stunning medieval frescoes ("Triumph of Death"), Roman sarcophagi. Far fewer tourists than the Tower.
Must-SeeUnderrated
2Pisa Cathedral (Duomo)
Romanesque masterpiece (1063). Giovanni Pisano's extraordinary carved pulpit. Galileo's lamp. Free with any Piazza dei Miracoli ticket.
Architecture
3Piazza dei Cavalieri
Medieval political heart redesigned by Vasari. Palazzo della Carovana (now Scuola Normale Superiore). Torre della Fame — where Count Ugolino starved (Dante's Inferno).
HistoryDante
4Baptistery of St. John
Italy's largest baptistery. Extraordinary acoustics — guards demonstrate the 7-second echo every 30 min. Nicola Pisano's carved pulpit (1260), precursor to the Renaissance.
Acoustics
5Santa Maria della Spina
Tiny jewel-box Gothic church on the Arno (1230). Named for a thorn from Christ's crown. Dismantled and moved stone-by-stone in 1871. Exquisite carvings.
Jewel Box

💎 Hidden Gems

1Keith Haring "Tuttomondo" Mural
One of Haring's final works (June 1989). 180 sq meters, 30 figures representing world peace. On the wall of Church of Sant'Antonio near the station. Free.
Hidden GemStreet Art
2Orto Botanico di Pisa
Founded 1544 — the OLDEST university botanical garden in the world. Quiet, shaded oasis away from tourists. Historic greenhouses. ~4 EUR.
Hidden GemOldest in World
3Lungarni at Sunset
Walk the Arno riverbanks from Ponte di Mezzo westward. Colorful palazzi reflected in water, golden light. Where locals do their passeggiata.
Hidden GemSunset

🍝 Food

1Il Montino — Cecina
Since 1927. Pisa's signature street food: cecina (chickpea flatbread). Order "cinque e cinque" — cecina stuffed inside focaccia. Cash only, locals queue daily.
Must-TrySignature Dish
2Osteria Bernardo
Authentic Tuscan trattoria. Mucco pisano (local braised beef), ribollita, pappardelle al cinghiale. ~25-35 EUR pp with wine.
Traditional

🛶 Venice — La Serenissima

Days 7-8 · 2 days · Floating city of 1000 years

⭐ Classic Historic Sites

1Basilica di San Marco
11th-century Byzantine masterpiece with 8,000+ sq meters of gold mosaics. Represents 1,000 years of Venetian power. Book skip-the-line (€3). See the Pala d'Oro and Loggia dei Cavalli.
Must-See
2Palazzo Ducale (Doge's Palace)
Gothic seat of Venetian government for 700+ years. Tintoretto's "Paradise" (largest oil painting in world). Book the "Secret Itineraries" tour — prison cells, Casanova's escape route.
Must-SeeSecret Tour
3Rialto Bridge & Market
Iconic 1591 stone bridge. The fish market (Pescheria) has operated since 1097, still active Tue-Sat mornings. Living Venetian food culture since a millennium.
MarketMorning
4Scuola Grande di San Rocco
"The Sistine Chapel of Venice." 60+ Tintoretto paintings (1564-1588). He spent 23 years here. Bring binoculars for ceilings. Less crowded than San Marco.
ArtUncrowded
5Basilica dei Frari
Massive Gothic church (1338). Titian's "Assumption of the Virgin" (1518), his tomb, Bellini's triptych, Canova's pyramid tomb. Multiple masterpieces.
Masterpieces
6Torcello Island
The "mother island" of Venice, settled 5th-7th centuries. Cathedral (639 AD) has stunning Byzantine mosaics including Last Judgment. Where Venetian civilization began. Vaporetto 12, combine with Burano.
Half DayOrigins

💎 Hidden Gems

1Scala Contarini del Bovolo
Hidden Renaissance spiral staircase (1499) tucked in a tiny courtyard. Most tourists walk right past. Architecturally unique, stunning 360° panorama from rooftop.
Hidden GemArchitecture
2San Giorgio Maggiore (Campanile)
Palladio's church on the island facing San Marco. THE best view of Venice — better than San Marco's tower — with almost no queue. 5-min vaporetto. Also has a Borges-inspired labyrinth.
Hidden GemBest View
3Jewish Ghetto (Ghetto Novo)
The FIRST ghetto in the world (1516 — the word originates here). 5 synagogues hidden in plain sight. Tallest buildings in Venice (built upward due to space constraints). Guided tours reveal stunning interiors.
Hidden GemHistoric
4Libreria Acqua Alta
Bookshop where books are stored in gondolas and bathtubs to protect from flooding. Back exit opens onto a canal. Staircase made of encyclopedias. Utterly unique.
Hidden GemUnique
5Squero di San Trovaso
One of the last gondola boatyards, operating since 17th century. Watch gondolas being hand-built. Looks like an alpine chalet (builders came from the Dolomites). View from across the canal.
Hidden GemLiving Craft

🍝 Food

1All'Arco (Cicchetti Bar)
San Polo, near Rialto. Best cicchetti in Venice — crostini with baccalà mantecato, sarde in saor. Father-and-son team using market ingredients. Go ~11am.
Must-TryAuthentic
2Bacaro Crawl (Self-Guided)
THE Venice food experience: 4-5 bars, 2-3 cicchetti + ombra at each. Route: All'Arco → Cantina Do Mori (since 1462) → Do Spade → Cantina Gia Schiavi → Al Timon. Budget €25-35.
ExperienceAuthentic
3Caffè Florian
Oldest café in continuous operation in the world (1720). Casanova, Dickens, Goethe all sat here. Expensive (€15+ coffee) but once-in-a-lifetime atmosphere. Go inside the ornate rooms.
HistoricSplurge
4Osteria alle Testiere
Tiny (9 tables) seafood restaurant, one of Venice's best. Raw scallops, spaghetti with clams. No pizza, no meat — pure Venetian seafood. MUST book weeks ahead.
Fine DiningBook Ahead

👔 Milan — Design & Innovation

Days 9-10 · 2 days · Italy's modern powerhouse

⭐ Classic Historic Sites

1Duomo di Milano + Rooftop
Gothic masterpiece, 600 years in the making (1386-1965). Climb to rooftop terraces for stunning views and close-up spire details. Allow 2-3 hours with rooftop.
Must-SeeRooftop
2The Last Supper (Santa Maria delle Grazie)
Leonardo da Vinci's masterpiece (1495-1498). UNESCO site. Only 25 people per 15-min slot. Book weeks/months ahead on official site. Life-changing experience.
Must-SeeBook Months Ahead
3Castello Sforzesco
15th-century fortress. Houses Michelangelo's final sculpture (Rondanini Pietà), Bellini, Mantegna, Canaletto. Free courtyard; museum ticket covers all.
Michelangelo
4Galleria Vittorio Emanuele II
Italy's oldest shopping gallery (1877). Stunning iron-and-glass architecture. Spin on the bull mosaic for luck. Camparino bar (where Campari was first served).
FreeArchitecture
5Pinacoteca di Brera
Italy's finest art: Raphael's "Marriage of the Virgin," Mantegna's "Dead Christ," Caravaggio. In the charming Brera district.
Art
6Teatro alla Scala
World's most famous opera house (1778). Visit the museum even without a show. Check for last-minute performance tickets.
Culture

💎 Hidden Gems

1San Maurizio al Monastero Maggiore
"Milan's Sistine Chapel" — breathtaking 16th-century frescoes covering every surface. FREE entry, relatively uncrowded. Mind-blowing quality.
Hidden GemFreeMust-See
2Navigli District Canals
Remnants of Milan's canal system (designed partly by da Vinci). Naviglio Grande still flows. Best at sunset. Antique market last Sunday of month.
Hidden GemEvening
3Highline Galleria (Rooftop Walk)
Walk on the glass rooftop of the Galleria Vittorio Emanuele II. Stunning unusual Duomo views. Book at highlinegalleria.com.
Hidden GemViews
4Chiesa di San Bernardino alle Ossa
Chapel walls decorated with human bones and skulls (dating to 1210). Near the Duomo but overlooked by most. Free entry. Hauntingly beautiful.
Hidden GemMacabre
5Fondazione Prada
Rem Koolhaas-designed art space in converted distillery. Bar Luce designed by Wes Anderson. Haunted House tower in 24-karat gold leaf.
Hidden GemContemporary

🍝 Food

1Trattoria Milanese
Since 1933. Definitive Milanese food: Risotto alla Milanese (saffron), Cotoletta alla Milanese (veal cutlet). White tablecloths, local clientele.
Traditional
2Luini Panzerotti
Legendary since 1888, near the Duomo. Panzerotto: fried dough pocket with mozzarella + tomato. Cheap, iconic street food. Line moves quickly.
Street FoodInstitution
3Navigli Aperitivo Experience
Milan's sacred aperitivo hour (6-9 PM): buy a drink, get free buffet food. Terrazza Aperol (Duomo view), Ceresio 7 (rooftop), canal bars.
ExperienceFree Food
4Pasticceria Marchesi (est. 1824)
Milan's oldest pastry shop (now Prada-owned). Panettone year-round, exquisite pastries, Art Nouveau interiors. Perfect for breakfast or merenda.
Historic

🏔️ St. Moritz & Engadin Valley — Alpine Paradise

Days 11-12 · 2 days · Swiss mountains, Romansh culture, and glacier views

⭐ Top Experiences

1Muottas Muragl (2,456m)
The most iconic viewpoint in Upper Engadin. Funicular up. Overlooks ALL FIVE Engadin lakes. Panoramic restaurant and romantic mountain hotel. Sunset here is transcendent.
Must-SeeViews
2Diavolezza Glacier Panorama (2,978m)
Cable car brings you face-to-face with Piz Palü, Piz Bernina (4049m, highest in Eastern Alps), and Morteratsch Glacier. One of the most dramatic high-alpine vistas accessible without climbing.
Breathtaking
3Sils Maria & Nietzsche House
Where Nietzsche spent 7 summers (1881-1888) and wrote "Thus Spoke Zarathustra." Village between Lake Sils and Lake Silvaplana. Beautiful sgraffito houses.
PhilosophyCulture
4Segantini Museum
Largest collection of Alpine painter Giovanni Segantini (1858-1899). His monumental "Alpine Triptych" (Life, Nature, Death) is the centerpiece. Purpose-built memorial dome.
Art
5Zuoz — Medieval Engadin Town
Best-preserved medieval town in the valley. Main square with sgraffito houses, Planta palace. Strong Romansh cultural identity. Less touristy, more authentic.
ArchitectureCulture
6Bernina Railway (UNESCO) — The Journey
Milan→Tirano→St. Moritz. 196 bridges, 55 tunnels, crosses 2,253m. Brusio circular viaduct, Alp Grüm, Lago Bianco. The journey IS a top experience. Sit LEFT side.
UNESCOScenic

💎 Hidden Gems

1Val Roseg
Pristine glacial valley from Pontresina. Walk (1.5h) or horse-drawn carriage through larch forests to glacier. Surrounded by 4000m peaks. Car-free, completely unspoiled.
Hidden GemNature
2Val Fex
Secret car-free walking valley from Sils Maria. Gentle 1-hour stroll through traditional farmhouses. Preserved in original state for centuries. Feels like stepping back 200 years.
Hidden GemTimeless
3Lake Staz (Lej da Staz)
Hidden forest lake between St. Moritz and Celerina. 20-min walk through woods. Perfectly calm, surrounded by larch and pine. Swimming in summer. Minutes from town, world away.
Hidden GemSecret
4Guarda Village (Lower Engadin)
Best-preserved Engadin village with extraordinary sgraffito wall decorations. Inspired "A Bell for Ursli." Tiny, quiet, photogenic beyond belief. ~45 min drive.
Hidden GemPhotogenic

🍝 Food

1Stüva Colani (Madulain)
Acclaimed restaurant in 500-year-old Engadin house. Refined Capuns (chard-wrapped dumplings), wild game, homemade pasta. Intimate pine-paneled stüva rooms. Reserve ahead.
Fine Dining
2Muottas Muragl Panoramic Restaurant
Eat Bündnerfleisch, Engadiner Nusstorte, Pizokel, and fondue while gazing at five lakes. The view elevates everything. Take the funicular up.
Views
3Restaurant Roseg Gletscher
Reached only on foot or horse carriage in Val Roseg. Simple Alpine food in glacial valley setting. Rösti, soups, local meats. The journey IS the experience.
Adventure
4Confiserie Hanselmann (since 1894)
St. Moritz's oldest pastry shop. Buy Engadiner Nusstorte (caramelized walnut tart) — the signature Engadin dessert. Perfect souvenir.
PastrySouvenir

🍺 Craft Beer Guide — Italy & Switzerland

Italy has one of Europe's most exciting emerging craft beer scenes. Here's your city-by-city guide.

🏛️ Rome

1

🍺 Open Baladin

📍 Via degli Specchi 6 (near Campo de' Fiori)
The pioneer of Italian craft beer culture in Rome. 40+ taps plus 100+ bottles. Baladin is one of Italy's most important craft breweries (Piedmont-based). Excellent food menu too. Lively, central, beer-nerd heaven.
🎯 Try: "Nazionale" (Italian-ingredient lager), "Isaac" (Belgian wit with spices), "Xyauyù" (barrel-aged barley wine — extraordinary)
2

🍺 Ma Che Siete Venuti A Fa' ("The Football Pub")

📍 Via Benedetta 25, Trastevere
Tiny, legendary — one of Europe's best beer destinations. ~15 rotating taps featuring Italy's & world's best craft breweries. Extremely knowledgeable staff. Standing room mostly; spills onto the street.
🎯 Try: Whatever's freshest from Brewfist, Extraomnes, CR/AK, or Birrificio Italiano
3

🍺 Birra Più

📍 Via del Pigneto 105 (Pigneto neighborhood)
Bottle shop + taps in Rome's hipster/alternative neighborhood. Focus on cutting-edge Italian microbreweries. Pigneto has great street art and restaurants too. Relaxed, local, discovery-oriented.
🎯 Try: Ask for small Italian producers you won't find elsewhere
4

🍺 Brasserie 4:20

📍 Via Portuense 82 (near Trastevere)
Brewpub with house-brewed beers plus guest taps. Known for hop-forward styles (IPAs, APAs) and experimental brews. Good kitchen with burgers. Industrial-chic, younger crowd.
🎯 Try: Their seasonal IPAs and house pale ales
5

🍺 Birra del Borgo (Lazio brewery, Rome presence)

📍 Various locations — check for current taproom
From Borgorose in Lazio. World-class beers, one of Italy's most celebrated craft breweries. Their "ReAle" was a milestone in Italian craft brewing. Look for them on tap across Rome.

🏔️ St. Moritz & Engadin

The Engadin beer scene is small but authentic — mountain breweries using glacial water create unique Alpine beers.

1

🍺 Engadiner Brauerei

📍 Celerina (3 minutes from St. Moritz)
The local craft brewery, literally next door to St. Moritz. Brews with mountain spring water at 1,800m altitude. Range of craft beers including flagship lager and seasonal specials. Available at most local restaurants.
🎯 Try: Their flagship lager and seasonal Alpine beers — ask for "Engadiner Bier" at any restaurant
2

🍺 Bieraria Tschlin

📍 Tschlin village, Lower Engadin (~1 hour from St. Moritz)
Claims to be the HIGHEST brewery in Europe (1,500m). Organic craft beers brewed with pure mountain spring water in a tiny Romansh village. Unique Swiss-Romansh brewing culture. Worth a detour if visiting Guarda/Lower Engadin.
🎯 Try: Their organic mountain lager — tastes like glacial purity
3

🍺 Bobby's Pub & Roo Bar

📍 St. Moritz center / Hotel Steffani
Bobby's: Classic pub with good beer selection, casual, popular with locals. Roo Bar (Hotel Steffani): Laid-back with solid craft selection and cocktails. Both are your best in-town evening options.
🎯 Try: Engadiner Bier on tap + any Swiss craft guest selections

🍺 Italian Craft Beer Pro Tips

  • 🇮🇹 Italy has ~1,800 craft breweries — it's one of Europe's fastest-growing beer countries
  • 🍇 Italian Grape Ale (IGA) — a uniquely Italian style using wine grapes in beer. Try it!
  • 🌰 Chestnut ales — another Italian specialty using local chestnuts. Autumn seasonal.
  • 🍺 Italian Pils — Italian brewers make exceptional pilsners (Tipopils, Birra del Borgo's Lisa)
  • 💰 Craft beer in Italy: expect €5-8 per pint at dedicated bars
  • 🍷 In Venice: embrace Spritz and ombra (small wines) — that IS the local culture
  • 🏔️ Swiss craft beer: generally more expensive (CHF 7-12) but brewed with incredible water
  • 🏆 Italy's "Big 5" breweries to seek everywhere: Baladin, Birra del Borgo, Birrificio Italiano, Lambrate, Brewfist

St. Moritz → Rome

🍝 Universal Food Rules

❌ NEVER eat at restaurants with picture menus, tourist menus, or touts outside
☕ Espresso at the BAR (standing) = €1-1.50. At a TABLE = €5-12. Stand like a local.
🍦 Gelato: look for "produzione propria" and natural colors. Avoid mountain-piled neon gelato.
🍷 Aperitivo hour (6-9 PM) = buy a drink, often get free buffet food (especially Milan)
💧 Tap water is drinkable in Italy. Public fountains everywhere (Rome has ~2,500 nasoni)
🧾 "Coperto" (cover charge, €1-3) is normal and legal in Italy. Not a scam.

🎒 Packing & Preparation

👟 Comfortable walking shoes (you'll walk 15-25k steps/day on cobblestones)
🧥 Layers for St. Moritz (1,800m altitude, cool even in summer)
⛪ Shoulders & knees covered for churches (carry a scarf/shawl)
🔌 EU Type C/L plugs (Italy uses both, Type L has 3 inline pins)

⚡ Power Moves & Time Savers

🎫 Skip-the-line: Always pre-book Vatican, Uffizi, Accademia, Borghese, Last Supper, Colosseum underground

🎁 Surprise Recommendations — My Personal Picks

1. San Clemente, Rome
The 3-layer church is genuinely one of the most mind-blowing things in Italy. 12th century → 4th century → 1st century Mithraic temple with running underground river. Better than most "famous" sites.
2. Bernina Railway (the journey itself)
Don't treat it as mere transport. This UNESCO heritage train ride through glaciers, Alpine lakes, and circular viaducts is a TOP-5 experience of the entire trip.
3. Bacaro Crawl in Venice
Skip expensive sit-down restaurants. Hop 4-5 bacari eating cicchetti and drinking ombre. €25-35 for the most authentic, memorable Venetian food experience possible.
4. San Maurizio al Monastero Maggiore, Milan
FREE entry to what locals call "Milan's Sistine Chapel." Jaw-dropping frescoes on every surface. Almost no tourists. Better than many paid museums.
5. Val Fex horse carriage, St. Moritz
A car-free valley accessible only on foot or by horse-drawn carriage. Feels like stepping 200 years into the past. Pure Alpine magic.
6. Trapizzino in Testaccio, Rome
A genius invention: triangular pizza pockets stuffed with classic Roman fillings. €3-5 each. The best cheap food innovation in modern Roman cuisine.
7. Brancacci Chapel, Florence
Where painting was reinvented. Masaccio's 1425 frescoes changed everything — Michelangelo came here to study. Only 30 people at a time. Intimate, revolutionary.
8. Muottas Muragl at sunset
Watch the sun set over five Alpine lakes from 2,456m. Stay for dinner at the panoramic restaurant. If clear, this is the single most beautiful view of the trip.
